About Our Church

Pilgrim Church began in 1917 when a group of people felt the need to organize a congregation for the German Protestants in the Labadie Community. The first services were held on the lawn of Mr. and Mrs. Florenz Drewel with Rev. J.N. Schuch, pastor of St. Peter's, Washington, officiating. The congregation sat on seats made of planks laid on wooden boxes and nail kegs, and gasoline torches supplied the light with a table as a pulpit. Eventually services were held in the Labadie Hall. In November 1917, a constitution was adopted, and when the charter was closed in January 1918, the congregation numbered 71 communicants. By March, a Sunday School was organized with 8 teachers and confirmation classes started. It was not until 1926 that a building was erected on property on Academy Hill purchased for $800 by the faithful efforts of the Ladies' Dorcas Society.

In the same year, Pilgrim Church was officially accepted as a member of the Evangelical Synod of North America and in 1934, became part of the Evangelical and Reformed Church. A parsonage was built next to the church in 1950. Pilgrim became part of the United Church of Christ in 1957. For many years, Pilgrim was served by students from Eden Theological Seminary. In October 1938, Rev. A.H. Behle became the first resident pastor. During the following years, resident ministers and students continued to serve Pilgrim, each leaving a part of himself with the congregation. Lay leadership has always played an important role in the ministry of the church, both locally and conference wide. Currently serving the church is Rev. Francis Grantz, a bi-vocational pastor. “The Diamond Year of God's Grace, Today, Tomorrow and Always” was the theme chosen for the 75th Anniversary of Pilgrim.
